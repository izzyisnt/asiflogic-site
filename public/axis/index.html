Instrument design · scan axis

The physics wrote the spec, and then it vetoed the mechanism.

A hydrophone that maps an acoustic field has to carry a sub-wavelength receiver through a tank and know where it was. This page is the specification for that motion axis — derived from the field physics rather than chosen from a catalogue — and the verdict on the flexure the design plan proposed to build it from. Every number below is computed by the modules the page is generated from; none of it is typed in twice.

top frequency 40 kHz step 9.5 mm accuracy ±0.32 mm travel 396 mm 10 proofs, each with a control nothing measured yet
Status: specification only Nothing here has been built or measured. The tank, the probe elements and a multimeter that can read resistance are all still unbought. What this page contains is a set of falsifiable claims about a machine that does not exist — which is the point at which they are cheapest to be wrong about.

1. The probe sets the top frequency and it is not the one the plan assumed

Everything about a scanned measurement scales from its shortest wavelength, so the top frequency is the first decision and every other number follows from it. It turns out not to be a decision at all. The needle probe already carries a proven constraint — its finished 6 mm tip has to stay a Rayleigh scatterer (ka ≤ 0.5) or it stirs the field it is trying to read, which is the exact fault that disqualified the larger hydrophone it replaced. Solve that threshold for frequency instead of checking it at one, and it returns 40.32 kHz.

The design plan proposed mapping across a 20–60 kHz band. At 60 kHz the same tip runs ka = 0.74 and, because scattered power goes as the fourth power of ka, perturbs the field about 5× harder than it does at the specified band top. Nothing was measured to find this: it is one existing proof evaluated at a frequency nobody had tried.

3. The mechanism where the plan and the physics part company

The plan's thesis was that tolerance is the product: build the axis as a monolithic flexure — one piece of metal, cut so that thin sections bend elastically — and you get no backlash, no stiction and no wear, with repeatability set by material hysteresis rather than by mechanics. Because a flexure hinge's stiffness goes as the cube of its thickness, the manufacturing tolerance on that one dimension dominates whether the finished instrument behaves as designed, and buying a tighter process becomes a real engineering argument rather than a preference.

That argument is sound, and the arithmetic behind it checks out at 10.9×. The trouble is that this instrument needs 396 mm of travel, and a flexure's range is bounded by its own yield stress.

So the axis is a lead screw on a linear rail with an anti-backlash nut, carriage above the waterline, probe on a thin stem. The derived requirement is ±0.32 mm over 396 mm, which that class of hardware holds with an order of magnitude in hand. The interesting engineering in this instrument is not the axis.

Where the flexure argument is right It is a megahertz-class argument. Push the same phase budget up in frequency and the position tolerance falls under ten microns at about 1.27 MHz, and mechanics with backlash and stiction stop being an option. The plan's thesis was not wrong — it was aimed at a different machine.

4. The probe holder is part of the position budget a defect this page shipped with, and the fix

The first version of Figure 1 put the projector flat on the tank floor and hung the probe from the rail down to a scan plane one standoff above it. That is 630 mm of 6 mm tube — a slenderness of 105:1 — and it was not a decision. It was a consequence of where the rail and the plane had been drawn, and nothing had checked it.

Checking it: at the design traverse of 200 mm/s that stem deflects 443 µm under hydrodynamic drag. The entire position budget is 317 µm. The probe holder alone overspends the whole budget by 40%, before the axis contributes any error at all. Worse, its first bending mode is 11.5 Hz and vortex shedding locks onto that at 0.34 m/s — not a comfortable distance from a 200 mm/s traverse, and lock-in turns tens of microns of bend into millimetres of oscillation.

The stem cannot be made thicker. Six millimetres is a ceiling the Rayleigh proof in §1 imposes — a fatter holder stirs the field it is holding the probe in. Stiffness has to come from length instead, and the only way to shorten the stem is to lift the projector off the floor. A 300 mm pedestal takes the stem to 330 mm: 13× the tip stiffness, first mode 41.7 Hz, drag deflection 33 µm — 11% of the budget instead of 140%.

And then re-probe the fix Raising the projector is a change to the acoustics, so it has to be checked against them rather than assumed harmless. The scan plane lands 380 mm off the floor in a 550 mm fill, leaving 170 mm to the free surface against the 107 mm the direct-arrival criterion demands — it passes, and prove_raised_projector_keeps_its_clearances has a control that lifts it until it does not.

5. The 10× what two vendor pages did to it

The claim was that wire EDM at ±0.005 mm buys an order of magnitude more predictable stiffness than milling at ±0.05 mm. Two facts read off the vendor's own pages, after the plan was written, take most of it away.

First, the default tolerance on a small feature is not the advertised one. The CNC page advertises ±0.05 mm, but the ordering guidelines state that ISO 2768 class m applies when no tolerance class is given, and that a tighter figure applies only to specifically annotated regions of a 2D drawing. ISO 2768-m over 0.5–3 mm is ±0.1 mm — twice the headline. On a 0.5 mm hinge that is ±80% of stiffness, so a hinge ordered as a STEP file with nothing else is looser than the plan's own milled control.

Second, this vendor's wire EDM is a roughing-class cut. The same guidelines give wire-cut and EDM features a surface roughness of ≥Ra6.3 µm. The trade figure the plan used — Ra 0.2–0.8 µm — is what EDM gives with skim passes; ≥Ra6.3 is what it gives without them. On a half-millimetre section, roughness is thickness uncertainty, and cubed it comes back as about ±20% of stiffness — which swamps the ±5% the tight dimensional tolerance was bought to deliver. The vendor commits to no EDM dimensional tolerance at all; the ±0.001 mm quoted on its blog is described as industry capability, not as a service level.

The procurement finding The physics sells the flexure. This vendor, ordered from as its pages currently read, does not deliver it. What the thesis needs is a shop that skim-passes and commits to a dimensional tolerance in writing — and that is a question to ask before a quote, not after a part.

7. What is not checked stated so it cannot read as covered

The moment "unknown" reads as "fine", a proof is a rubber stamp. These are the gaps this round did not close.

The axis

  • the 3-degree phase budget is [X] -- a design choice with nothing in the repo behind it. Every derived number scales with it
  • mechanism accuracy and backlash are CLASS typicals [I]; no leadscrew stage has been chosen, quoted or datasheet-read
  • Abbe error is unmodelled: the probe hangs on a stem below the carriage, so carriage pitch/yaw multiplies by the stem length at the tip. At a 300 mm stem the 0.31 mm budget needs ~0.06 deg of angular stability, which is easy for a rail and is still not CHECKED here
  • no thermal term. Water sound speed moves 2.4 m/s/K (antinode_stage), so the wavelength every number here divides by drifts ~0.16 %/K -- a temperature-controlled tank is in scope and unproven
  • motor drive coupling into the charge amp is Phase 3 and entirely unaddressed: a stepper chopping a few amps beside an AD743 charge amp is the obvious cross-domain failure and deserves its own proof
  • the reconstruction check is 1-D on a line of samples; a real raster is 2-D and its second axis can alias independently
  • the tank, the probe elements and a working multimeter are all still WANTED rows in INVENTORY.md -- this is a specification, and NOTHING in this module has been measured

The flexure

  • EDM_TOLERANCE_UNCOMMITTED: JLCCNC lists EDM and wire cutting as processes but publishes no dimensional tolerance for them [M]; +/-0.005 mm is a trade typical [I]. Get it in writing from JLCDFM or support before any hinge is designed to it -- this is the plan's Phase-0 gate, and reading the pages SHARPENED it rather than closing it
  • Rz/Ra ~ 5 is [X] with no ISO basis; the 4-7x spread moves the roughness band from 16% to 29% and `roughness_band_over_rz_range` reports both
  • the roughness-as-thickness-uncertainty model is [X] -- it is dimensionally right and its coefficient is not measured
  • Al 7075-T6 E and sigma_y are handbook values [I], not a mill certificate; 7075 in thin section is also notch-sensitive and that is unmodelled
  • fatigue is entirely unmodelled: a scan axis cycles, and the stress allowable here is a static yield figure with a safety factor of 2 [X], not an endurance limit
  • no thermal term: a monolithic aluminium flexure moves ~23 ppm/K and the tank is temperature-controlled to a budget nothing here checks
  • JLCCNC's minimum wall and minimum feature size remain unpublished on both sites -- whether they will cut a 0.5 mm section AT ALL is unanswered
