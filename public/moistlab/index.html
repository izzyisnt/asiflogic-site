The pack charges. It just never balances.

A high-voltage BMS controller, mid-build. This page exists so the state of the work is legible from outside it — including the parts that are still soft. Every claim below carries the mark it earned.

System GoodWe GW7600A-ES + CATL/ZN stack Pile ~768 kWh LFP, unbuilt Phase decisions made, bench gate open

The finding

The inverter is configured to charge this pack to 116.8 V. It stops at 108.3 V. That is not a fault, nothing alarms, and almost no capacity is lost — which is exactly why it went unnoticed.

The cost is hidden one layer down. The cell balancers only switch on above roughly 3.40 V per cell. The pack tops out at 3.39. It approaches the threshold and never crosses it, so the balancers have — as far as the data goes — never run.

The part I got wrong

LFP's charge curve is famously flat, so a coulomb-counting module BMS reaches "100 %" while its cells are still near 3.39 V. The inverter believes the state of charge and tapers off. That much holds.

The first version of this page went further and claimed the cells were visibly drifting apart as a result — 12 mV to 33 mV in four days. That comparison was invalid and the claim is withdrawn. One sample was taken while charging, the other while discharging, at different states of charge. Cell spread moves with current and position on the curve, so those two numbers were never comparable to begin with.

What survives is the ceiling itself, which is measured: the pack does not reach the voltage at which its balancers switch on. What does not survive is any claim about the consequence. Whether the imbalance is actually growing is now an open question needing a series recorded at matched current and state of charge — which is what the monitor now records every minute.

The design consequence is unchanged and still holds. The controller being built aggregates many modules and reports a deliberately conservative state of charge while charging. That policy terminates a charge earlier than the factory system does, so it would deepen this problem rather than fix it, unless charge termination carries an explicit condition to hold until the balance window is actually reached.

Settled this week

M

It speaks GoodWe's own protocol

Not Pylontech HV, which was the working hypothesis. Pylontech HV is 29-bit frames at 500 kbps; this bus is 11-bit at 250 kbps, and the Pylontech ID space has no 0x456. Three independent mismatches.

M

The menu multiplier is a voltage divided by 48

The cryptic Default*N setting is GoodWe's module count: nominal volts ÷ 48, integer part. 102.4 ÷ 48 = 2.13 → 2. The inverter's own register reads 2. Matches three field reports out of three.

M

Two battery families, not interchangeable

The salvage market conflates them constantly — listings carry one family's part number under the other's name. The auction split 355 controller heads of one kind from 694 of the other, and the modules went to separate lots.

I

The unlisted battery was never hiding

Absence from the compatibility list looked like a mystery. The manufacturer's own preamble says unlisted batteries speaking its protocol exist. It didn't need a listing.

Where it is soft

The rest of this page reads tidier than the work is. These are the places a reader should keep their weight off.

The first backup was fiction

Reading a block of settings from an address I chose myself returned neatly formatted filler — values that looked exactly like configuration and were not. Caught only by overlapping two reads that disagreed. The file is kept, renamed so it cannot be restored by accident.

194 registers still unread

The good backup covers the settings that matter and is verified read-for-read. It is not a complete image, and calling it one would be the same mistake in a different coat.

Some of the instrument trouble was self-inflicted

Two of my own processes were contending for one half-duplex serial port and I read the resulting dropouts as hardware flakiness for longer than I should have.

A corroborating number was retracted the same day

The cell-drift figure above was published, then withdrawn hours later when the two samples turned out to have been taken in opposite states. It was caught by a monitor that now records the conditions alongside every reading — which is the only reason it was caught at all.

The ceiling's cause is not yet separated

Whether the pack stops at 108 V because the battery advertises that limit, or simply because current tapers away, is undetermined. A per-minute logger is now running to settle it against daylight.

What landing it looks like

The shape of the finished thing is not in doubt; the evidence under it is what's being assembled. Three commitments carry over from the parts already decided.

  • Every load-bearing claim becomes a test that can fail. The balance-window miss above is being written as a check with the factory behaviour frozen as its counter-example — a real bug found in the field, not an invented one.
  • Protection does not rest on software. The disconnect fails open in hardware; firmware supplies a heartbeat and an intention, never the last word.
  • Nothing is its own witness. The aggregation logic is built twice, in two languages, and checked against the other — the same reason the bad backup was caught at all.

Robustness here means the page you're reading gets shorter in the soft section and longer in the settled one, and that the difference is always visible.
