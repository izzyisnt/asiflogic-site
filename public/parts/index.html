logic-gated LED strobe head + constant-current driver (3.3 V trigger in)

test equipment / optics · added 2026-07-28

For the stadium-trough surface-wave rig — the optical counterpart to the acoustic TR chain, imaging the SAME cavity geometry in 2-D where `natural_superoscillation.py` currently only models it. SPEC IS SET BY COHERENCE, NOT BRIGHTNESS [I]: must accept an external logic gate so the strobe and the wave drive come off ONE crystal (the ESP32 already on the buy order can emit both with a programmable phase offset) — a free-running strobe gives a beat, a locked one with a stepped offset gives a chosen phase of the field, which is the whole measurement. **THE OFFSET MUST BE STEPPABLE, NOT MERELY LOCKED** [added 2026-07-28 with the acquisition proofs]: under a single-tone drive the surface is η = Re[ψ·e^{iωt}], so each strobe offset samples the cycle at every pixel at once, and K evenly spaced offsets recover the complex field ψ by one DFT bin — no Hilbert transform, no 2-D analytic signal, no phase unwrapping. **Buy K = 6 offsets minimum.** Two is the tempting economy and is catastrophic (`two_step_control`): it returns exactly 2·Re[ψ], destroying all phase, and the vortex counter still reports a plausible count within ~10% of the true one, so the failure is SILENT. Four — the textbook phase-shifting algorithm — is destroyed by the 3rd harmonic, which a nonlinearly driven paddle has (`four_step_control`); it fails on every odd harmonic ≥3. Six rejects harmonics 2, 3 and 4. This is a SLOW strobe problem: surface waves in a 2×2×6 trough run ~5–30 Hz (period 33–200 ms), so even a 1 ms flash is <3% of a cycle — no xenon, no high-speed head, an LED array + MOSFET/constant-current driver is sufficient and cheap. Do NOT buy a self-contained tachometer strobe with only a knob: no external sync = no phase control = qualitative pictures only. NB the strobe alone yields shadowgraph (intensity ∝ surface curvature, ∂²η), NOT the phase field — vortex charge and local wavenumber need η(x,y), so pair it with Free-Surface Synthetic Schlieren: random dot pattern under the surface (refraction displaces the apparent dots ∝ ∇η), camera above, integrate for η. Dot pattern is a printed sheet, cost ~0. **DOT-PLANE DEPTH [CORRECTED 2026-07-28, same day]**: this row originally said to lay the pattern on the tank FLOOR, reasoning only that an opaque galvanized bottom rules out imaging from below. That is wrong and the correction stands in place of it — FS-SS sensitivity scales with the dot plane's depth below the free surface, so at a full 0.55 m fill the reference wave shifts dots 4.35 mm against a 5 mm pattern, past the 2.5 mm ambiguity ceiling, and the reconstruction folds WITHOUT COMPLAINING. Mount the dot plane ~150 mm below the surface on a submerged rack (≈0.40 m off the floor) → 1.19 mm shift, 6.5 px, comfortable. Frozen as `moistlab/acoustics/ripple_rig.py::floor_pattern_control`, a proof that fails on exactly the original recommendation. That module also found the constraint this row could not have: drive amplitude has a TWO-SIDED window, 1.25–105 µm at the reference geometry — the ambiguity ceiling wants a small wave, and the blind disc around each vortex core (where amplitude sinks into the noise and the charge becomes unreadable) wants a big one. Criteria, controls and the reference geometry — 0.35 m patch, **25 Hz**, λ 9.93 mm, 50 µm amplitude, 3902 vortices in frame: `ripple_rig.py` (12 proofs, `ripple_rig_unchecked()` for the gaps — including that the random-wave model is assumed, and is the thing the rig exists to test). RS-485 link-integrity kit (termination + bias + isolated transceiver)

electronics / comms · added 2026-07-30

Driven by a MEASURED fault: the laptop USB-RS485 dongle on /dev/ttyUSB0 showed a **~45% garble rate on a known-good register** (canary = 45352, must read 1168; failed 9 of 20 reads, 2026-07-30). Suspects in order: (1) missing **120 R termination** across A/B at each bus end — 2 needed, not one per node; (2) missing **idle fail-safe bias** (a pair of resistors holding A>B when no driver is active — without it an idle bus floats and noise reads as start bits, which produces exactly the CRC-mismatch signature seen); (3) **no galvanic isolation** on a cheap CH340 dongle sharing a ground with a switching HV inverter → common-mode excursions beyond the transceiver's -7/+12 V input window. ROOT CAUSE NOW KNOWN [M operator]: the existing run is **untwisted, unisolated, ~20 ft** beside a switching inverter — twisting IS the common-mode rejection mechanism, so an untwisted pair has none. AS-BUILT DEFECT [M operator 2026-07-30]: "multiple cables going through one hole now." **Unblocks the DHCP takeover, which is MEASURED to be impossible any other way.** Router is confirmed **Starlink Gen 3 with RJ45 LAN ports** [M operator], so Bypass mode is available in the app and **no Starlink Ethernet Adapter is needed** — but there are **no ethernet cables on hand** [M operator], which is the hard blocker. The cheap alternative was tested and FAILED: a second DHCP server on the Pi (`moistlabpower`, 192.168.1.204) racing the Starlink router **lost 27 of 27 trials** measured from a real client with a raw DHCPDISCOVER probe (`~/tools/dhcprace.py`, also on the Pi at `~/dhcprace.py`) — Starlink ~1.7–2.9 ms vs Pi ~3.1–9.5 ms. **Structural, not tunable: the Starlink router IS the access point**, so it is always one radio hop closer than a wifi-attached Pi. Two real but insufficient levers were found and are worth keeping: dnsmasq **`no-ping`** (the default pre-offer ICMP probe stalls the offer **3006 ms** — without it the Pi loses even from its own vantage point) and **wifi powersave off** (`nmcli con mod <con> 802-11-wireless.powersave 2`; RTT avg 4.29 -> 3.63 ms). Also measured: the two DHCP pools **overlap across the whole /24** (both servers offered .233 in one trial) and Starlink's range cannot be constrained, so a coexisting server risks duplicate addresses with no upside once it loses. BUY: 2x Cat5e patch cables (one Starlink LAN -> new router WAN, one spare for the Pi's unused eth0) + an OpenWrt-class router (GL.iNet or a used TP-Link). THEN: stage the new router in double-NAT, clone SSID **STARLINK** (the CNC ESP32 has `$Sta/SSID=STARLINK` baked in, so cloning lets it rejoin untouched), flip Bypass in the app, serve 192.168.1.0/24 with reservations, hand out **.204 as DNS**, and delete `server=/lan/192.168.1.1` from the Pi's `/etc/dnsmasq.d/moistlabpower.conf` (it points at a router that disappears in bypass). A cable alone also enables a cheaper *test* — cable the Pi into a LAN port and re-race — but a race decided by ~1 ms is fragile and is not a foundation for the shop LAN
