The Needle Hydrophone

Thin needle for dark noodle. — design note, 2026-08-01

A sub-wavelength underwater probe for two jobs a conventional cylinder hydrophone is provably wrong at: reading the true peak of a focused ultrasound field, and mapping the fine structure — including the lines of perfect silence (acoustic vortices) — of a diffuse field at 32 kHz, where the wavelength in water is 47 mm. Every load-bearing claim below is a machine-checked proof in our repository, each shipped with a deliberately broken control that its test must catch. Provenance labels: [M] measured or transcribed from a primary source · [I] inferred · [X] modeled.

Element: a 5 × 0.4 mm PZT (SM111) disc with factory wire leads [M], radial resonance 450 ± 10 kHz — seven times above our band, where the 26 mm cylinder it replaces resonates inside the band. The front end is a charge amplifier at the dry end: its scale factor is immune to the element's ±15% capacitance tolerance and to the cable — which is why the electronics can be evicted from the tip at all. (A tiny buffer could be potted at the tip, as our cylinder probe does inside its much roomier element — dry-end charge sensing is a choice for scale stability and a minimal tip, not a geometric necessity.)

Two aperture findings

1. For a wave field confined to a plane (ripples on a water surface), a round sensor of any size multiplies the whole map by one Bessel factor, 2J₁(kR)/(kR) — it cannot move a single vortex. We measured the gain against that formula and it tracks to better than 1% — including the spectacular part: at exactly R = 0.61λ the factor crosses zero and the sensor goes completely deaf, with perfect maps on either side of the null. Ears and mouths obey the same math: run backwards as a source, that disc is mute along its own plane.

2. For a plane slice of a 3-D field — the real mapping mission — incidence angles spread the in-plane spectrum, the same kernel reweights it non-uniformly, and a 26 mm aperture destroys a fifth of the vortex map while the 6 mm tip keeps all of it. Finding 1 cannot be extrapolated to finding 2; we tested both.

What is deliberately not claimed

Sensitivity is an envelope, 1.2–10 µV/Pa [I], until our reciprocity coupler measures it — where the built probe lands depends on the potting. Every absolute number above uses the worst case.

Mounted on the tube, the disc becomes a rim-clamped bender with an estimated flexural mode near 111 kHz in water [X] — close enough to color the band top by ~3 dB. Our own flatness proof covers the free element only; the built probe gets an impedance sweep before its response is trusted. This flaw was found by attacking our own design, which is the house method.

Scatter suppression is a Rayleigh (ka)⁴ scaling [I], not yet a measured field perturbation.

The absolute-pressure path (dosimetry)

Mapping needs only where the field is silent; dosimetry — "how many pascals did the sample actually receive" — needs an absolute probe, and below 100 kHz that is a genuine metrology desert: commercial precision hydrophone calibrations start at 0.25–0.5 MHz, the one cavitation probe covering our band publishes no uncertainty figure, and even NPL's primary method carries 7–12%. Our answer is a calibration chain traceable to geometry and electrical measurement only — no calibrated reference anywhere in it:

 rung 0  reciprocity itself: machine-proved from energy conservation
         (piezo_theory rung 3) — the theorem the method leans on
 rung 1  closed rigid water cavity, every dimension ≪ λ:
         p/Q = K_water/(jωV) — impedance fixed by GEOMETRY [M]
         two-transducer reciprocity in the coupler, 1–8 kHz
 rung 2  built-in pistonphone: micrometer piston, known ΔV
         → p = K·ΔV/V — a second absolute route, same cavity
 rung 3  transfer 1–8 kHz → 32.2 kHz via the measured BVD model;
         verify free-field against the cylinder probe at range
         (reciprocity again: J = 2r/ρf — geometry again)

This ladder is what collapses the needle's 1.2–10 µV/Pa sensitivity envelope to a number with an uncertainty budget. It runs in parallel with the mapping build — machining the coupler is the long pole — and it is the instrumentation core of the Asiflogic program: absolute in-situ dosimetry for low-frequency ultrasound. (Known trap, learned on the cylinder probe and written down so it stays learned: a slow-dunk static check works on a high-impedance buffer but a charge amp high-passes it away — the coupler rungs are the road.)

Context

Commercial needle hydrophones (0.5–1 mm elements) are specified for 0.25–10 MHz — above our band. The calibrated cavitation probe that covers 20 kHz–1.2 MHz publishes no uncertainty figure at low frequency. Sub-100-kHz underwater metrology is a calibration desert; this probe, and the geometry-traceable coupler that will calibrate it, are steps toward filling it.
